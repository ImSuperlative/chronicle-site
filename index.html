A journal that leaves you the files.

Chronicle is a journal for iPhone. It writes what you save as ordinary files in ordinary folders — readable, copyable, and yours, with or without Chronicle.

Coming to the App Store

Your journal is a folder

Most journal apps keep your writing inside a database only they can open. Chronicle keeps a folder, organised by date, holding plain text, JSON, images, PDFs, and GeoJSON routes. You can open it on a computer, browse it in Files, copy it to a drive, or move it somewhere else entirely. Nothing about it needs Chronicle to make sense.

Delete and reinstall Chronicle, reconnect a destination folder, and your entire journal comes back.

No account, no server

There is no sign-up and no Chronicle server. Nothing you write is sent to us, because there is no us to send it to. Chronicle collects no analytics and no crash reports.

To keep the journal in sync or backed up, you point Chronicle at a folder you already own — in iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or anywhere else that appears in the iOS Files app. That provider syncs the folder, under your account and their terms. Chronicle never sits in the middle.

A day, as you actually lived it

A day is made of entries. An entry can hold text, and anything else worth keeping:

  • Photos from Apple Photos or Google Photos, saved as metadata, a thumbnail, or the full original — your choice
  • Workouts from Health, with their GPS routes if you want them
  • Places, searched and pinned
  • Documents from anywhere in Files — a scan, a receipt, a page from a reMarkable

Chronicle suggests; it never decides. It won't attach a photo, a workout, or a document on its own, and it never deletes an original from Photos, Health, or a connected service.

Built for the long run

  • Offline first. The local copy is always complete. Edits don't wait for a network.
  • Portable formats. Markdown, JSON, GeoJSON, PDF, and ordinary image files. Nothing proprietary.
  • Redundant by design. One folder syncs; any number of others take full backups.
  • Careful with locations. Precise GPS routes stay on your iPhone until you specifically say otherwise.
